Every colour and pattern

All of it drawn by the same generator that builds the textures the game ships. Nothing on this page is an artist's impression.

A paint job is one of three things: a flat colour, a stencil in two colours you pick, or an advanced camo pattern in one of twelve fixed colour schemes. Each part of a weapon and each piece of clothing carries its own.

Read those numbers honestly. The mod contains 47 colours and 60 camo tiles; a fresh character holds four colours and one stencil. Everything else is earned — stencils from cans, camo from the military hunt, extra colours from event codes. The 10 810 figure is how many two-colour tiles are pre-rendered in the download, not how many you can paint on day one.

The palette — 47 colours

Four colours are free and always in your palette. The rest are granted: today that means promo codes from events and giveaways in Discord, and the founder colour that five players will earn. Colours you do not hold are simply not drawn in the in-game menu, so the grid you see there is always the grid you can use.

Any colour works as a base and any colour works as a stencil accent — there are no colour-only-solid restrictions. That costs the mod a pre-rendered texture for every base × accent pair, which is why the palette grows slowly and deliberately.

Stencils — you pick both colours

Five stencils, learned one at a time from Pattern Paint Cans. Every player starts knowing Stripes |; the other four are found in industrial loot and stay yours for the rest of the wipe. The in-game menu files them under Basic patterns — learned from cans.

Pick a base and an accent below and the five previews redraw. These are the real tiles — same geometry, same random seeds as the textures on your jacket.

Tiled, the way it lands on an item

A single tile is 256 px square and repeats seamlessly across whatever it is painted on. Pick a stencil above to see it repeated — closer to what you actually get on a backpack. Showing: Checker.

A stencil is one fixed tile. The same 256 px tile goes on a balaclava, a jacket and a backpack, so a stencil comes out bigger or smaller depending on how that item's UV stretches it. Only the advanced camo patterns ship in three densities the item picks for itself — how the tiers work.

Advanced camo — 5 patterns × 12 schemes

Advanced patterns are multi-colour camouflage. You do not pick their colours: each one comes in fixed schemes of three or four tones, the way real camo does. Picking one in the menu replaces both colour grids with a single Camo scheme row. They need an Advanced Paint Kit to apply.

What you collect is the combination. A Military Paint Can teaches exactly what is printed on its label — one pattern in one scheme. “Thicket in Autumn” tells you nothing about “Tiger in Autumn”.

Six of the twelve schemes are obtainable right now: UA Pixel, Woodland, Autumn, Desert, Urban and Night. UA Pixel comes with the Advanced Paint Kit for all five patterns; the other five are what Military Paint Cans teach — 25 combinations make up the collection. The remaining six schemes are built and shipped in the mod but never spawn; they are held back for events and awards.

Camo density adapts to the item. Unlike a stencil, each advanced pattern ships three tiles — loose for small gear, tighter for garments, tightest for backpacks — so the camo reads at roughly the same real-world size wherever it goes. You do not choose it; the item does.

The twelve schemes

Each scheme is a fixed set of tones. The first is the background, the rest are the shapes drawn over it.
